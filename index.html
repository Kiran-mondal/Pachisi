Rules of Pachisi

  • Players: Minimum 2, Maximum 4 players. Pachisi is played in an anti-clockwise direction[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span).
  • Tokens: The game involves 4 sets of 4 counters[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span).
  • Pasha (Dice): Played with a pair of stick dice. A doublet allows moving a pair of counters together, or as individual counters[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).
  • Safe Zones: Squares 1 to 7 are called the 'Belly[span_3](start_span)'[span_3](end_span). A counter cannot go into the bellies of other counters[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
  • Capturing: A counter is captured when a player's counter lands on the other player's counter in the same square[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).
  • Winning: The first player to get all his 4 counters home is the winner[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

The Heritage of Chaupar

Pachisi and Chaupar are ancient Indian board games that were famously played by the Maharajas and Royals. The history of games dates to the ancient human past, capturing the ideas and worldviews of their cultures and passing them on to the next generation[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).

Pachisi, dating back to roughly the 6th century, gave rise to the globally popular game Ludo[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span). They hold a significant place in Indian mythology. By playing this game today, you are preserving a piece of a 5,000-year-old tradition.
