📜 Rules of Pachisi & Chaupar

👥 Players: Played between 2 to 4 players. Movement progresses anti-clockwise around the board.

🎯 Objective: The ultimate goal is to move all four of your tokens out of the starting yard and safely enter the Center Home.

🎲 Pasha & Movement: Movement is determined by rolling dice. Doublets grant an Extra Turn!

⭐ Safe Zones: Certain squares are 'Safe Zones'. If your token is on a Safe Zone, it cannot be captured.

⚔ Capturing: Landing exactly on an opponent's token sends it back to their yard and gives you an extra turn.

👑 The Royal Heritage

The National Game of India: Pachisi is an ancient cross-and-circle board game that originated in Ancient India (around the 4th century). It is the direct ancestor of modern games like Ludo.

Epic Mythology: Variations of this game, particularly Chaupar, hold a legendary place in Hindu mythology, famously known as the dice game in the Mahabharata.

Played by Mughal Emperors: The great Mughal Emperor Akbar played the game on a massive scale at his palaces. The courtyard was designed as a giant board, and courtiers were moved around as living tokens!
