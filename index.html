Rules of Pachisi & Chaupar

  • Players: Played between 2 to 4 players. Movement progresses anti-clockwise around the board arms.
  • Token Base (Yard): Tokens start in each player's respective Token Yard in the board corner.
  • Pasha (Stick Dice): Played with solid wooden stick dice. Doublets allow moving tokens and grant an extra turn!
  • Safe Zones (Pachisi): Marked squares with a golden star protect tokens from being captured.
  • Chaupar Mode: Safe zones are completely disabled! Every square on the board becomes a battleground where capturing can occur.
  • Capturing: Landing directly on an opponent's token sends their token back to their Yard and grants you an Extra Turn.
  • Winning: The first player to safely navigate all 4 tokens around the full perimeter and into the central HOME square wins!

The Royal Heritage of Chaupar & Pachisi

Pachisi and Chaupar are ancient Indian board games that were famously played by Maharajas, Emperors, and Royals. Dating back to as early as the 4th to 6th century, these games hold a significant place in Indian mythology and history—most notably featured in the epic saga of Mahabharata.


Mughal Emperor Akbar was known to play Pachisi on a grand scale in his palace courtyard at Fatehpur Sikri, using living court maidens as human pawns moving across giant inlaid stone squares! Historically, Pachisi also served as the primary ancestor to the modern western board game Ludo.
